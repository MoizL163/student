## How I'm Approaching Each Area

### Iteration & Loop Tracing

This gets day one because it's my biggest gap. My approach is to trace loops manually using a step-by-step table instead of running through them in my head. I'll work through the three AP CSP loop types — `REPEAT n TIMES`, `FOR EACH item IN list`, and `REPEAT UNTIL` — and spend extra time on robot grid navigation questions, which require the same careful sequential thinking.

### Math Expressions & Modulo

I'm keeping this simple. My plan is to memorize the three modulo patterns that show up most on AP CSP — even/odd checks, wrap-around cycling, and last-digit extraction — so I can recognize them quickly instead of re-deriving them each time. I'll also make sure I have integer division vs. modulo straight in my head.

### Random Values & Algorithm Development

Two specific things I'm locking in: that `RANDOM(a, b)` is inclusive on both ends (which is different from Python's `range()`), and a consistent method for tracing unfamiliar algorithms by identifying inputs, process, and output before trying to answer. I'll practice this on several problems I haven't seen before.

### Distributed & Parallel Computing

I understand the concepts — my issue is the calculation questions. I'm drilling the speedup model specifically: sequential time is the sum of all tasks, parallel time is the duration of the longest task, and dependent tasks can't be parallelized. A handful of focused problems should be enough to lock this in.
